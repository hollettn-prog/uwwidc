PEPROF 222

Building a Positive Learning Environment

Teacher behaviors that help learners feel safe, known, respected, and ready to participate

PEPROF 2221
OPENING REFLECTION

Start with a place where you learned well

What did the leader actually do?

Avoid describing the person as only “nice” or “good.” Name an observable action, routine, phrase, or response.

PEPROF 2222
WHAT STUDENTS NOTICE

Before learners engage, they are reading the environment

01Am I safe here?
02Do I belong here?
03Can I succeed here?
04Can I trust the adults and peers?
PEPROF 2223
COMPARE THE OPENING

The same activity can create two very different experiences

Opening A

Equipment is scattered
Directions begin while students talk
Teams are selected publicly
Mistakes receive visible correction

Opening B

Equipment and boundaries are ready
A practiced signal gains attention
Grouping is quick and private
Correction protects dignity

PEPROF 2224
TEACHER DECISION POINTS

A positive environment is built before, during, and after activity

BEFOREPlan for access
DURINGNotice participation
AFTERCheck understanding and follow through
PEPROF 2225
FOUR VISIBLE PRACTICES

Care becomes meaningful when learners can observe it

KNOWUse names accurately and learn what matters to students.
NOTICEAttend to effort, hesitation, exclusion, and change.
RESPONDAdjust support while protecting dignity and expectations.
FOLLOW THROUGHDo what you said you would do—consistently and fairly.
PEPROF 2226
EQUITY IN ACTION

Fairness is responsive—not identical

Same for everyone

Same distance
Same equipment
Same performance expectation

Same goal, responsive paths

Adjust space, task, equipment, or support while preserving the learning goal

PEPROF 2227
CONSISTENCY CREATES SAFETY

Trust grows when the teacher is predictable

Expectations are taught
Rules are applied fairly
Feedback is respectful
Promises are kept
PEPROF 2228
PE, HEALTH, AND APE

Movement spaces require deliberate belonging

Participation is highly visible—and comparison is easy.
Grouping can communicate status, acceptance, or exclusion.
Bodies, identities, abilities, and skill histories are present.
Choice can increase access without lowering the learning goal.
PEPROF 2229
SMALL-GROUP APPLICATION

Analyze one routine through the learner’s eyes

What might help learners feel safe, known, and included?

Then identify uncertainty, status, or exclusion—and propose one small teacher move.

PEPROF 22210
CLOSING REFLECTION

Leave with one move you can use immediately

Learners are more likely to feel known, safe, and included when I…

Write one observable teacher action—not a personality trait.
