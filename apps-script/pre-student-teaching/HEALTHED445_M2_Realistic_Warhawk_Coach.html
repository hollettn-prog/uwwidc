What Would Count as Learning?

Discussion

A learning task should give you evidence that students can do what your learning goal describes. Share your draft, help a classmate consider their plan, and use the feedback to strengthen your own.

How the Pieces Connect

1. Learner NeedWhat might students need?
2. NHES IndicatorWhich skill guides the plan?
3. Learning GoalWhat should students learn?
4. Task & EvidenceHow will they show it?

1Share Your Draft

Choose a Consumer Health context and a grades 9–12 NHES performance indicator. Describe a plausible learner need. Label it as an assumption for planning; you do not need to collect data from a school.

Share your learning goal and a short description of the task you would use to check it. Explain what an individual student would say, write, or demonstrate.

Where are you unsure the plan fits together?

Give classmates a question they can help you work through.

2Respond to One Classmate

Read the goal alongside the proposed task. Identify one part that fits well and suggest one useful change. Explain how your suggestion would help the teacher see whether students learned the intended skill.

3Use the Feedback

Carry this draft into your Needs-to-Evidence Plan. Revise it using your classmate’s feedback or explain why you kept a planning choice.

Start with the evidence

What would one student actually say, write, or demonstrate that would convince you they met your learning goal?

Test the alignment

  • Does your task require the skill named in your NHES indicator?
  • Could a student complete the task without demonstrating that skill?
  • Which part of your plan would you most like a classmate to question?

Example of the thinking process

Imagine a Consumer Health lesson about evaluating online health claims. A teacher might ask students to identify a trustworthy source. But if the goal is to evaluate claims, would simply naming a website show enough evidence? What could a student explain about the claim and source to make their thinking visible?

Your turn: Apply that same check to your own goal and task. This is an illustration, not a draft to copy.
