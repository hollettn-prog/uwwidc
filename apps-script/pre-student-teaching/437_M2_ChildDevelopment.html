KINDERGARTEN & GRADE 1

What should you know about younger learners?

Add a few useful notes for each area. You are building a quick-reference guide, not writing four mini-essays.

Physical

Think about coordination, balance, movement skills, body control, strength, and endurance.

Cognitive

Think about attention, directions, memory, rules, and how much information students can process.

Social

Think about sharing, taking turns, cooperation, peers, and working with others.

Emotional

Think about confidence, frustration, independence, self-regulation, and the need for encouragement.

What does this mean when you're teaching?

Give 2–3 things you might do because you are teaching students in K–1.

Need a few examples?
  • Keep directions short and clear.
  • Demonstrate instead of only explaining.
  • Use equipment students can control successfully.
  • Build in lots of practice opportunities.
  • Use simple, predictable routines.
  • Give quick and specific feedback.

These are examples, not a checklist. Choose ideas that connect to the developmental characteristics you identified.

GRADES 2 & 3

What's changing for these learners?

Students are becoming more capable and independent, but they still need clear instruction and plenty of opportunities to practice successfully.

Physical

Think about improving coordination, combining skills, control, strength, and endurance.

Cognitive

Think about understanding rules, remembering sequences, problem-solving, and beginning strategy.

Social

Think about fairness, cooperation, partners/groups, and the growing importance of peers.

Emotional

Think about confidence, frustration, feedback, and comparing themselves with others.

What does this mean when you're teaching?

What are 2–3 choices you might make for students in grades 2–3?

Need an example?

Students may be ready for more complex tasks or simple decision-making, but they still benefit from clear demonstrations, appropriate challenge, and plenty of successful practice.

GRADES 4 & 5

What should you consider with older elementary students?

Students may be ready for more complexity, choice, and responsibility. Differences in skill, confidence, and awareness of peers may also become more noticeable.

Physical

Think about skill combinations, coordination, strength/endurance, and differences in ability.

Cognitive

Think about strategy, problem-solving, decision-making, and understanding performance.

Social

Think about peers, belonging, teamwork, leadership, responsibility, and competition.

Emotional

Think about self-consciousness, confidence, comparison, success/failure, and perceptions of ability.

What does this mean when you're teaching?

Give 2–3 choices you might make for students in grades 4–5.

Need a few ideas?
  • Offer meaningful choices or challenge levels.
  • Introduce simple strategies and decision-making.
  • Use leadership roles appropriately.
  • Avoid public comparisons of ability.
  • Think carefully about how competition is structured.

ONE LAST THING

Students in the same grade can still need different things

Grade-level characteristics are useful for planning, but they are not rules about every child.
