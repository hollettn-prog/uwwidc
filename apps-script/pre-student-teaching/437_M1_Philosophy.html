My Elementary PE Philosophy

This is a chance to think about what matters to you as an elementary PE teacher. You do not need to have everything figured out yet. Your philosophy will continue to develop as you learn more and gain teaching experience.

WHAT MATTERS TO YOU?

When elementary PE is going well, what are students getting from it?

Think about what you want students to learn, experience, and carry with them because they participated in quality physical education.

Jot down your thinking. You do not need to make this sound like a formal philosophy statement yet.
Need a few ideas to get started?

You might think about:

  • developing movement skills
  • building confidence
  • enjoying movement and physical activity
  • learning to work with others
  • trying new things
  • understanding their bodies
  • developing healthy habits
  • feeling included and successful
  • wanting to stay active outside of PE

You do not need to include all of these. Focus on what actually matters to you.

YOUR THREE BELIEFS

What do you want to guide your teaching?

Choose three ideas that feel important to you. For each one, think about what that belief could actually look like when you are teaching.

Not sure what counts as a belief?

Try asking yourself:

  • What should PE feel like for students?
  • How important is skill development?
  • What role should enjoyment play?
  • How should students with different abilities experience PE?
  • What should students learn besides physical skills?
  • What do I want students to believe about physical activity?

TRY IT OUT

How would your beliefs affect a teaching decision?

There is not one correct response here. Use the ideas you have been developing to think through the situation.

You have limited PE time

You teach elementary PE twice each week for 30 minutes. Someone tells you:

“With that little time, just keep the kids moving and make sure they have fun.”

Activity and enjoyment certainly matter. What else would you want to make sure students are learning or experiencing during those 30 minutes?

What would you prioritize, and why?
Need a prompt?

You could think about:

  • What would you want students to learn?
  • What would you want them to experience?
  • How would you balance learning, activity, and enjoyment?
  • Is there anything you would not want to sacrifice?
YOUR PHILOSOPHY

Put your ideas together

You have already done most of the thinking. Now bring those ideas together into a short philosophy you could share with a cooperating teacher, principal, or future employer.

Keep it manageable

Aim for about 2–3 paragraphs. Explain what you believe elementary PE should accomplish, what you value for students, and how those beliefs influence the way you want to teach.

Sentence starters if you want them

Use any of these if they help:

  • I believe elementary physical education should...
  • I want students to leave PE feeling, knowing, or being able to...
  • One thing that is especially important to me is...
  • As a teacher, this means I will...

You do not have to use these. They are simply here if staring at a blank page is not helping.

Before you save it

Read your philosophy one more time and ask:

  • Does this sound like me?
  • Can someone tell what I value for students?
  • Can someone tell how my beliefs might affect my teaching?
  • Did I explain my ideas rather than just list educational terms?
