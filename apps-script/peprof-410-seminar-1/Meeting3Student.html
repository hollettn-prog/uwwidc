Coach's Corner

“I Think...” Is a Starting Point.

“My directions are too long.”

“This class takes forever to transition.”

“I think this adaptation is helping.”

“They aren't engaged.”

Before changing everything: What would you need to see, hear, count, time, ask or compare?
A Useful Cycle

Try → Gather → Think → Adjust

TRY

Make one intentional change.

GATHER

Collect a little useful evidence.

THINK

What does the evidence suggest?

ADJUST

What would you do next?

Not: Try → Be Brilliant Immediately.

Interaction 1

🔍 Teaching Mystery Lab

Each person gets a turn. Your team's first job is not to solve it.

Ask First

  • What is actually happening?
  • What are you noticing?
  • What might you be assuming?
  • When does it happen — and when doesn't it?
  • What information are you missing?

Interaction 2

🧪 Evidence Challenge

Question:
“I think my directions are too long and students stop listening.”

Your team decides:

  • What could you actually collect?
  • What would count as evidence?
  • What is one small adjustment you could try?
  • What would you check afterward?
08:00
Evidence Challenge Debrief

There Isn't One Perfect Measure.

⏱️ Time

Length of directions or transition.

👀 Observe

When attention begins to shift.

🔢 Count

Repeated questions after directions.

⚖️ Compare

One approach against another.

The useful question is: What evidence would help me make a better instructional decision?
Mini Teaching Experiment

Your Experiment Can Flop.

Seriously.

If your adjustment makes no difference, you still learned something. If it makes things worse, you learned something. If the evidence challenges your original theory, you definitely learned something.

We're grading the learning cycle — not whether your first idea was brilliant.
